# PawPixel: CRE client profile and research plan

Built with the CRE operating system (niche assessment workflow, Section 12) from the research in `reports/Viral pet video formats.md`. Every number below comes from that report and its sources; nothing here was checked on TikTok or Instagram. A human CRE must verify follower counts, views and post dates on-platform before an idea is used.

## Client profile

**Brand:** PawPixel. The app turns a photo of your real dog, cat or rabbit into a pixel pet. It lives in a little room on your phone and on your home-screen widget, and its mood follows the real care you log.
**Platforms:** TikTok and Instagram Reels. Brand accounts aren't set up yet; the research suggests 3 to 5 themed accounts (reveal, setup, character) rather than one.
**Niche:** Pixel-art virtual pets of your real pet, combined with daily pet-care reminders.
**Audience:** Pet owners 18 to 40 in the US and Europe, plus the Philippines (the pilot city is Naga). They like cozy games, Tamagotchi nostalgia and home-screen setups. Rabbit owners are a separate group with their own content habits (#bunnytok care routines).
**Format:** Faceless. The footage is your own pet on your phone, plus screen recordings of the app and home screen. Talking-head is optional for the founder story.
**Editing:** Beginner to intermediate: phone and CapCut. The instructions give short CapCut steps.
**CTA ladder:** Follow + Save now. Once the app is in the stores, use "Comment PIXEL" for the request series, and later "Link in bio" for the download.
**Tone:** Warm, playful, cozy. The pet speaks in its own voice. Never preachy or guilt-tripping about care.
**Hard no:**
- Fake pet-cam or AI "doorbell" footage (these were fact-checked in 2025).
- Scare, kidnap or wall "tests" that animal-welfare experts criticise.
- Reposting other creators' clips. Instagram stops recommending accounts that do this.
- Trending sounds on the brand account. Business accounts must use TikTok's Commercial Music Library or the Meta Sound Collection.

**Traps (three specific to PawPixel):**
1. **Health-claim trap.** PawPixel reminds you about care; it doesn't make pets healthier. "Your pixel dog gets grumpy when you skip the walk" is fine. "Keeps your dog healthy" or "prevents illness" is not, and it breaks TikTok ad policy.
2. **One-off AI filter trap.** "Turn your pet into X" peaks within weeks, and portrait apps built on it barely registered. Every reveal has to point at what happens next (the pet lives on and reacts), or it reads as just another filter.
3. **Seems-to-fit-but-doesn't trap: cozy-game and Tamagotchi content.** It's adjacent, but PawPixel is a care app, not a game. Borrow the shorthand ("a Tamagotchi, but it's my actual rabbit") without promising gameplay the app doesn't have.

**Hero story assets (two, to fill in):**
1. **Founder story:** why you built PawPixel, told with your own pet. Use the real reason only. *To fill in: the founder's own pet and the moment that started it.*
2. **First real owner:** a pilot owner's pet becoming a pixel pet and the owner's reaction. *To fill in after the likeness test (README, "Before you release").*

## Format guidance

Use your own pet's footage plus screen recordings. Keep videos 15 to 60 seconds and tease the payoff in the first second. Put text in the upper centre, in white Classic or Standard font. Use licensed audio or your pet's real sounds. Test hooks with Instagram Trial Reels and early TikTok posts before spending money. Then pay nano-creators to repeat the winning format and Spark-boost the outliers. Post 3 to 5 times a week on Instagram and more on TikTok.
