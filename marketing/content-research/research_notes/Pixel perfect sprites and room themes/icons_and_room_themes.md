Research date: 2026-10-13. Note on method: most primary pages (lospec.com, pixelparmesan.com, slynyrd.com, kenney.nl, itch.io, stardewvalleywiki.com, nookipedia.com, fandom wikis, wikipedia.org, gamedeveloper.com) were blocked by the research proxy (HTTP 403 on CONNECT), so their content was read through search-engine snippets rather than the full page. ## Key question 1: What grid size and rules do good 12x12/16x16 pixel icon sets follow?

### Takeaway
Good small icon sets are drawn at 16x16 (or 18x18 / 12x12), with 1-pixel-wide strokes, a 4-6 colour budget per icon, selective or coloured outlines rather than a hard black border, a single top-left light source, and silhouettes that are judged in a row at 1x before any detail is added; the recurring "broken" faults are jaggies (uneven staircase steps on diagonals and curves), doubles (an extra pixel that thickens a 1px line into a 90-degree corner), and orphan pixels (a lone pixel attached to nothing).

### Cited Findings

**Canvas and grid**
- 16x16 is described as "the practical size" for icons: "Below that most concepts stop being distinguishable, and above it icons start competing with the content they label." — [Divoom blog, "Why Pixel Art Still Works" (vendor blog; opinion)](https://divoom.com/blogs/industry-trends/pixel-art-still-works-4k-design-culture)
- Lospec's own browser pixel editor ships a "New 16x16 canvas" preset, i.e. 16x16 is the default starting canvas for small sprites/icons on Lospec. — [Lospec Pixel Editor](https://apps.lospec.com/pixel-editor/)
- Lospec hosts a walkthrough for small item icons at 18x18: "[Let's Pixel] 18x18 Item Icons" by Benjamin Anderson (HeartBeast), which walks through creating several small item sprites. — [Lospec tutorial listing](https://lospec.com/pixel-art-tutorials/lets-pixel-18x18-item-icons-by-heartbeast)
- Lospec also lists "What Size To Make PixelArt" by MortMort for choosing a canvas size, and "Pixel Logic" by Michael Azzi for lines, colours, anti-aliasing and dithering fundamentals. — [Lospec size-tag listing](https://lospec.com/pixel-art-tutorials/tags/size)
- Pixel Parmesan has a dedicated post on choosing the working resolution for pixel art. — [Pixel Parmesan, "Choosing the Right Resolution for your Pixel Art"](https://pixelparmesan.com/blog/choosing-the-right-resolution-for-your-pixel-art) (title and existence confirmed via search; page body not fetched)
- Stardew Valley's cursor/icon sheet (`Cursors.xnb`) is catalogued in 16x16 cells: e.g. arrow cursor at (0,0), hourglass at (16,0); "except for sprites with a clear 16x16 or similar box size, sprite sizes will show the minimum possible space for a sprite." — [Stardew Modding Wiki, Cursors](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/Cursors)
- Stardew's larger menu stat icons are 48x48 (e.g. `Energy.png`). — [Stardew Valley Wiki, Category: Menu icons](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Category:Menu_icons)
- Stardew Valley's world tile is 16x16 pixels (community forum answer). — [Chucklefish forum thread](https://community.playstarbound.com/threads/hi-i-want-to-know-the-size-of-the-stardew-valley-tiles-can-you-tell-me-thank-you.153101/)

**Outline (1px vs none), stroke, palette**
- Lospec's outline article: the baseline outline is one pixel wide; straight/horizontal lines are easy, diagonals and curves take more skill, and the fix is to make line segments "connect only diagonally". Small palettes make outlines important "because an object sharing a color with what's behind it blends in." — [Lospec, "Pixel Art Outlines"](https://lospec.com/articles/pixel-art-outlines)
- Part 2 covers coloured outlines: use a darker shade of the object's own colour as the outline; darker internal lines read as thicker and help suggest details smaller than a pixel. — [Lospec, "Pixel Art Outlines Part 2: Using Color"](https://lospec.com/articles/pixel-art-outlines-part-2-using-color)
- Against heavy black outlines on icons: they "vanish on dark backgrounds and look jagged on light ones"; the recommended approach is "selective outlining" or "colored outlines" where "you only put a dark pixel where a shadow would naturally fall." — [Divoom blog (vendor blog; opinion)](https://divoom.com/blogs/industry-trends/pixel-art-still-works-4k-design-culture)
- Palette budget: "using more than five or six colors for a single 16-pixel sprite usually results in a blurry, muddy mess"; suggested exercise: draw a 16x16 icon with only 4 colours (background, midtone, shadow, highlight). — [Divoom blog, "How Many Colors Should a Pixel-Art Palette Have?" (vendor blog; rule of thumb)](https://divoom.com/blogs/setup-ideas/pixel-art-palette-how-many-colors)
- Hand anti-aliasing: a 1-on/1-off pattern is the common way to draw diagonals; "use the same pattern for a given line thickness and angle." — [OpenGameArt beginner tutorial thread](https://lpc.opengameart.org/node/113983)
- Pixel Parmesan on AA: "AA utilizes subtle shifts in perceived value of pixels to create the illusion of 'breaking the grid'", and broken/selective outlines can convey where a surface turns away from the light. — [Pixel Parmesan, "Anti-Aliasing Fundamentals for Pixel Artists"](https://pixelparmesan.com/blog/anti-aliasing-fundamentals-for-pixel-artists)
- Lospec's "Creating Pixel Art" beginner tutorial lists the fundamentals as "antialiasing, dithering, clusters, lines, selective outlining, palette". — [Lospec UI-tag listing](https://lospec.com/pixel-art-tutorials/tags/ui)

**Silhouette / readability test**
- Peripheral-vision test: "Put your icon set in a row, look away, look back. Anything you have to think about has a silhouette problem." — [Divoom blog (vendor blog; opinion)](https://divoom.com/blogs/industry-trends/pixel-art-still-works-4k-design-culture)

**Consistent light direction**
- Top-left is "the most common light position" and "the default for games and illustrations"; keeping the light direction consistent within a work or game scene is a "Golden Rule", and mismatched light sources on characters, backgrounds and items "create visual dissonance". — [Pixnote, Shading & Lighting](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/shading)
- With a top-left light: "One side (the one directly facing the light) is the brightest, the second brightest is the top of the box, and the last side is the darkest"; placing the light behind or directly in front of the object "doesn't really work that well." — [Complete Guide to Isometric Pixel Art, ch. 3.2](https://students.northlake.wa.edu.au/StudentHTML/IsoPixArt/chapter3point2.html)
- Slynyrd's Pixelblog 6 is the dedicated light-and-shadow lesson in that series. — [Slynyrd, Pixelblog 6 - Light and Shadow](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/6/15/pixelblog-6-light-and-shadow) (page body not fetched)
- Pixel Parmesan: "a light source which hits the top face mainly as well as one side face partially is most effective", and "a light higher in the sky creates a shorter shadow." — [Pixel Parmesan, "Fundamentals of Isometric Pixel Art"](https://pixelparmesan.com/blog/fundamentals-of-isometric-pixel-art)
- Pixel Parmesan: "Palette decisions should be dictated to some extent by the lighting you are trying to convey"; value steps often "must be compressed to prevent banding and pillow shading." — [Pixel Parmesan, "Color Theory for Pixel Artists"](https://pixelparmesan.com/blog/color-theory-for-pixel-artists-its-all-relative)

**Common faults that make an icon look broken**
- Jaggies: a diagonal is a staircase that "only looks smooth when step lengths stay consistent"; uneven steps are the main cause of a broken edge. Orphan pixels: "a lone pixel poking off a shape with nothing connecting it"; fix by deleting or reattaching so every pixel belongs to a shape. — [Wayline, Pixel Art for Games lesson](https://www.wayline.io/learn/pixel-art/1)
- Doubles: extra pixels that thicken a 1px line and "create a 90 degree angle" and "make your art less neat and clear"; they often come from "alpha from image" conversions or dragging a brush instead of placing pixels; some apps offer a "pixel perfect" setting to avoid them. — [Clip Studio Tips, "Pixel Art Practices Explained Simply"](https://tips.clip-studio.com/de-de/articles/5360?org=1)
- Banding is "a common mistake among novice Pixel Artists—even folks with some great art talent." — [Daniel Silber, Pixel Art for Game Developers, ch. 10 (O'Reilly)](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/pixel-art-for/9781482252316/chapter-51.html)
- PixelJoint's community tutorial lists banding, jaggies and bad dithering as things to avoid. — [PixelJoint forum tutorial thread](https://pixeljoint.com/forum/forum_posts.asp?TID=11299)
- Curves: a counting method ("start at one pixel and add two, then three on each side, then count back down") keeps curve steps consistent; "the eye picks up small errors easily at this resolution, so each pixel should be placed on purpose." — [Wayline lesson](https://www.wayline.io/learn/pixel-art/1)

**Readability at 20-24 dp on a 400-450 ppi phone**
- Android density buckets: mdpi ~160 dpi (1.0x), hdpi ~240 (1.5x), xhdpi ~320 (2.0x), xxhdpi ~480 (3.0x), xxxhdpi ~640 (4.0x); `px = dp * (dpi / 160)`; 1 dp is roughly one pixel on a 160 dpi screen. — [Android developers, Support different pixel densities](https://developer.android.com/training/multiscreen/screendensities)
- Phones "commonly report a pixel ratio of 2 or 3 which indicates a Retina or high DPI display." — [React Native PixelRatio docs (Amazon mirror)](https://www.developer.amazon.com/docs/react-native-vega/0.72/pixelratio.html)
- Integer scaling is nearest-neighbour upscaling by a whole-number multiplier, "particularly useful for pixel art games that rely on sharp, blocky images"; "Both IS and NN preserve the clarity of the original image." — [LKML integer-scaling patch discussion](https://lkml.rescloud.iu.edu/hypermail/linux/kernel/2003.1/05492.html); overview at [Custom PC, "Integer scaling explained"](https://www.custompc.com/integer-scaling-explained)
- Export with "Nearest Neighbor" interpolation so edges stay crisp. — [Divoom blog](https://divoom.com/blogs/industry-trends/pixel-art-still-works-4k-design-culture)
- For pixel art textures: disable mipmaps and use "nearest" filtering only for actual pixel art. — [Defold forum reply](https://forum.defold.com/t/gui-texture-quality-when-scaled/65531/3)
- Mobile blur cause: "solutions that work on desktop don't seem to work on mobile devices because of the higher dpi" (browser scales the canvas to match dpi); fix by setting backing resolution explicitly. — [HTML5GameDevs thread (2016, dated)](https://html5gamedevs.com/topic/21555-nearest-neighbor-scaling-of-pixel-art-in-2016)

### Inferences
- Screen-pixels-per-icon-pixel on a 400-450 ppi phone: those phones fall in Android's xxhdpi bucket (~480 dpi, 3.0x) or iOS @3x, so 24 dp = 72 physical px. A 12x12 icon drawn into 24 dp is exactly 6 screen px per icon px (integer, crisp). A 16x16 icon fits 24 dp only at 4.5x (non-integer, will shimmer); at an integer 4x it is 64 px = 21.3 dp, and at 5x it is 80 px = 26.7 dp. So for a 24 dp button, 12x12 at 6x is the cleanest; for 16x16 pick a 20 dp (60 px = 3.75x, bad) or better a 21.3 dp / 26.7 dp slot, or let the icon box be sized in physical pixels (64 or 80) rather than dp. (Derived from the Android dp formula above; not stated by any source.)
- Minimum feature size: with 1px strokes and a 1-on/1-off AA pattern, the smallest readable feature is a 1px line with 1px of contrasting space around it; at 12x12 that leaves a 10x10 live area inside a 1px margin, so a glyph can afford roughly 3-4 strokes across. (Inference from the stroke and outline findings; no source gives a numeric minimum.)
- Symmetry: none of the sources state a symmetry rule explicitly; the jaggies/curve-counting advice implies symmetric curves should use mirrored step counts (e.g. 1-2-3-...-3-2-1) so a circle or heart reads evenly. Treat as practice, not citation.
- Which metaphors read instantly: no source ranks bowl, water drop, brush, pill, syringe, heart, paw, house, gear, camera, bell, share at 12-16 px. The silhouette row test is the only cited method; apply it. Of the requested metaphors, the ones with distinctive outer silhouettes (heart, house, bell, gear, paw, camera, drop) should pass the row test more easily than those that are thin or rectangular (brush, syringe, pill, share arrow), which depend on an interior detail; this is a judgement, not a sourced finding.

## Key question 2: Which pieces define each themed room, and what footprint sizes are typical in a 10x5-tile room?

### Takeaway
In Animal Crossing: New Horizons, footprints cluster hard at 1x1 (about half of housewares) and 2x1 (about 29%), with 2x2 at 11% and anything 3 wide under 5%; in Stardew Valley couches are 3 wide, TVs 2x2 or 3x1, rugs 2x1 to 5x3. Themed rooms are anchored by a few 2-wide pieces (sofa, table, bed, counter, bathtub) plus 1x1 filler and 1-2 wall pieces, and the theme is carried mostly by the wall and floor material.

### Inferences
- Mapping to PawPixel's 10x5 room (9-unit tiles): ACNH's size histogram suggests a catalogue of roughly 50% 1x1, 30% 2x1, 10% 2x2 and under 10% 3-wide pieces. In a 10-wide room, a 3-wide piece takes 30% of the width, so at most one 3-wide anchor per theme (sofa, gazebo/pond, bathtub-plus-vanity) is sensible; the Stardew couch (3 wide) and ACNH 3x3 gazebo/pool are the only common 3-wide anchors. Rugs should be 2x2 or 3x2 at most since 5x5 would cover half the floor.
- Suggested anchors per theme, with widths drawn from the ACNH/Stardew sizes above: living room = sofa 2-3 wide + low table 2 + TV/stereo 1-2 + floor lamp 1 + bookshelf 2 (wall or floor) + rug 3x2; playroom = toy chest 2, blocks 1, play tunnel/tent 2x2, ball pit (no sourced equivalent; ACNH plastic pool 2x2 is the closest) , rug 3x3, rocking horse 1, slide 2x1; garden = fence (wall row), flower beds 1-2, tree 2x2/3x2, pond 2x1-3x3, bird bath 1, lanterns 1, bench 2; kitchen = counter 2, stove 2 or range 1, fridge 1, island 2x2, pot rack 2 (wall), kitchen mat 2x1; bedroom = bed 2x1/2x2, end table 1, wardrobe 1, lamp 1 (misc), mirror 1, framed poster (wall); bathroom = tub 2x1, toilet 1, sink/vanity 1-2, towel rack (wall), bath mat 2x1, duck 1; attic/study = desk 2x1, chair 1, bookshelf/books, boxes 1-2, corkboard 2 (wall), typewriter/lamp 1; cafe = counter 2, café table 1 or 2x2, counter chair 1, dessert case 2, sign 1, bottle shelf 2 (wall), entrance mat 2x1.
- Wall vs floor: in ACNH 112 of 174 wall-mounted items are 1x1 and 29 are 2x1, so a wall catalogue of 1- and 2-column pieces (clock, poster, plaque, shelf, corkboard, pot rack, towel rack) matches the reference games; nothing wall-mounted is wider than 2 columns in the dataset.
- Theme palette rule of thumb (from the ACNH concept-to-wall/floor mapping): living/bedroom = wood (parquet, herringbone, pine-board, birch) + painted or panelled wall; playroom = puzzle/dot/block floors + playroom/quilt/cute wall; garden = lawn/meadow/gravel/dirt floors + fence/lattice/garden-vista wall; kitchen = retro tile or restaurant-kitchen floor + subway-tile/brick wall; bathroom = honeycomb/mosaic tile floor + tile wall; study/den = rosewood or plain floor + library wall; cafe = fast-food or natural-block floor + coffee-shop/diner/brick wall.

## Key question 3: How do games present a room-theme switch (wallpaper + floor + furniture set) to the player?

### Takeaway
Animal Crossing treats wallpaper and flooring as inventory items applied from a decorating mode (d-pad down, then X for inventory), sold daily by Nook's Cranny and Saharah, with accent walls, lighting and sound added in Happy Home Paradise; Stardew sells themed furniture through named catalogues (Furniture, Wizard, Junimo, Joja, Retro) and shows green/red placement cells; Cozy Grove groups decor into named themes and multi-item sets; Unpacking fixes the room type (walls, floor, built-in furniture) per level and lets the player only place items.

### Cited Findings
- ACNH, how to switch: you must have paid off the 5,000 Nook Miles debt and been upgraded to a homeowner; go home, press down on the d-pad to enter customization mode, press X to open the inventory and select the wallpaper or flooring. — [Twinfinite guide (2020)](https://twinfinite.net/guides/animal-crossing-new-horizons-wallpapers-rugs-change/)
- ACNH, where themes come from: Nook's Cranny "sells different wallpapers and floorings each day"; Saharah sells one mysterious wallpaper and one mysterious flooring per day, and five exchange tickets buy an extra flooring or wallpaper; wallpaper and flooring can also be crafted from DIY recipes. — [Twinfinite guide](https://twinfinite.net/guides/animal-crossing-new-horizons-customize-house-how/); [Animal Crossing Wiki, DIY recipes: wallpaper, rugs and flooring](https://animalcrossing.fandom.com/wiki/DIY_recipes/Wallpaper,_rugs,_and_flooring)
- ACNH accent walls: "another wallpaper is applied to one side of a room", unlocked with the Pro Decorating License or by playing Happy Home Paradise; removal is done from the wallpaper tab of the inventory while inside the house. — [Animal Crossing Wiki, Furniture (New Horizons)](https://animalcrossing.fandom.com/wiki/Furniture_(New_Horizons)); [GamersDecide, How to remove accent walls](https://gamersdecide.dev.weebpal.com/node/15978)
- Happy Home Paradise: set a feature wall by rotating the room so the wall faces you and pressing X; highlighting a placed item and pressing X cycles its colour variants; a lighting menu changes the room's colour and intensity; a separate soundscape menu; furniture sets from older games are included so "there's usually a theme that fits." — [Real Homes, HHP statement-piece guide](https://www.realhomes.com/features/how-to-design-a-room-around-a-statement-piece-in-happy-home-paradise)
- HHP wallpapers unlock by designing specific vacation homes and can then be bought from Wardell's catalog (example: 1,700 Poki). — [Nookipedia, White delicate-blooms wall](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Item:White_Delicate-Blooms_Wall_(New_Horizons))
- Series structure: in earlier Animal Crossing games a series or theme was roughly 10 furniture pieces with matching wallpaper and flooring; New Horizons removed themes, and series can now contain any furniture. — [Nookipedia, Furniture](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Furniture)
- In the ACNH dataset, series that still ship a matching wall + floor + rug triple include Bunny Day, Cinnamoroll, Hello Kitty, Kerokerokeroppi, Kiki & Lala, My Melody, Pompompurin, Turkey Day; Moroccan ships four walls and four floors; bamboo ships bamboo wall, bamboo-grove wall, bamboo flooring and bamboo mats/rugs; cherry blossoms ships cherry-blossom-trees wall, sakura-wood wall, cherry-blossom flooring and rug; diner ships four diner walls. — [Wallpaper.json / Floors.json / Rugs.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Norviah/animal-crossing/master/json/data/Wallpaper.json)
- The Patchwork Series comes in eight colour variations (Colorful, Vivid, Cool, Chic, Country, Nursery, Leaves, Cute); the Artful (formerly Zen Modern) Series was added in the 3.0 update; the Harmonious theme list pairs items with bamboo-grove wall and bamboo/gravel/tatami flooring. — [Nookipedia, Patchwork Series](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Patchwork_Series); [Nookipedia, Artful Series](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Zen_Modern_Series); [Nookipedia, Harmonious list](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Furniture/New_Horizons/Harmonious)
- Stardew Valley presents themed sets as separate purchasable catalogues: Furniture Catalogue, Wizard Catalogue, Junimo Catalogue, Joja Furniture Catalogue, Retro Catalogue, plus the wallpaper/flooring Catalogue; items from the Furniture Catalogue cost 0g once owned. — [Furniture Catalogue](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Furniture_Catalogue); [Wizard Catalogue](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Wizard_Catalogue); [Junimo Catalogue](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Junimo_Catalogue); [Joja Furniture Catalogue](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Joja_Furniture_Catalogue); [Retro Catalogue](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Retro_Catalogue); [Catalogue](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Catalogue)
- Stardew placement feedback: furniture "shows a green square where it can be placed and turns red where it can't." — [Stardew Valley Wiki, Furniture](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Furniture)
- Cozy Grove: decoration pages carry a "set" (e.g. Floral) and a "theme" (Rustic, Spooky); custom sets are crafted from two ingredients (Relaxing Campground: spice and softwood). — [Cozy Grove wiki, Decoration (Camp Spirit)](https://cozygrove.fandom.com/wiki/Decoration_(Camp_Spirit)); [Category: Relaxing campground](https://cozygrove.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Relaxing_campground)
- Unpacking: the player opens boxes and places items; "most items can go anywhere, with some hard restrictions, and the game flags misplaced items once a room is finished." — [Wikipedia, Unpacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unpacking_(video_game))
- Pokémon Gen II: room decorations are set through the bedroom PC and most positions are fixed, so the "theme switch" is a swap of bed, carpet and poster slots rather than free placement. — [Bulbapedia, Player's house](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/bedroom)

### Inferences
- The common pattern across ACNH, Stardew and Cozy Grove is: wall and floor are their own item types, swapped as a pair from an inventory/catalogue, with furniture sets sold or unlocked under a theme name; none of the sourced games applies a whole "room theme" in one tap, but ACNH's old ~10-piece series with matching wall + floor and Stardew's named catalogues are the closest precedents for a one-tap theme bundle (wall + floor + 8-12 pieces).
- For PawPixel, a theme picker that previews wall + floor + anchor pieces together, then applies them while keeping the pet's bed and bowl positions, would match the ACNH series mental model and Stardew's catalogue model; green/red tile feedback during manual placement is the established affordance.

- The Sims-style mobile decorating games were not covered; no reachable primary source turned up.
