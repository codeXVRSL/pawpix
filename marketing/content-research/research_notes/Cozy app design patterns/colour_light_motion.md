# Cozy app design patterns: colour, light, texture, typography and motion

Research date: 2026-10-12. Method note: in this environment direct page fetches (lostgarden.com, projecthorseshoe.com, gamedeveloper.com, m3.material.io, sketch.com, uppercutcrit.com, blakecrosley.com, podwise.ai) were blocked by the outbound proxy (403 / DNS). Everything below therefore comes from search-engine extracts of those pages plus secondary coverage, and each value names its source so the writer can judge weight. Where a value only appears in a third-party or AI-generated "design spec", it is flagged as weak. Nothing was invented; missing numbers are listed under Gaps.

Working definition used throughout: the Project Horseshoe 2017 workgroup (Lost Garden write-up by Daniel Cook et al., 24 Jan 2018) defines coziness as "how strongly a game evokes the fantasy of safety, abundance, and softness" — [Lost Garden, Cozy Games](https://lostgarden.com/2018/01/24/cozy-games/); [Project Horseshoe 2017 report section 3](https://www.projecthorseshoe.com/reports/featured/ph17r3.htm).

## Key question 1: Which concrete colour and light choices make a scene or screen read as cozy?

### Takeaway
Every source that commits to specifics converges on the same recipe: a warm, muted, low-saturation palette (cream/beige/soft brown/terracotta/honey), low ambient light with a warm local light source, no pure black or pure white, soft colour-tinted shadows and generous rounding. The one experimental study found that rounded corners alone measurably raise perceived "warmth" of an app. Exact hex values for the named games (Animal Crossing, Stardew, A Short Hike, Unpacking) were not published by their developers; the only developer-level colour statement found is that A Short Hike sampled its palette from photographs of the Canadian Shield in autumn.

### Cited Findings

Principles (Lost Garden / Project Horseshoe):
- Coziness is "how strongly a game evokes the fantasy of safety, abundance, and softness." "Softness" is the aesthetic signal of a low-stress space with a slower tempo and manageable scope; "cozy games use strong aesthetic signals that tell players they are in a low stress environment full of abundance and safety." — [Lost Garden, Cozy Games (2018)](https://lostgarden.com/2018/01/24/cozy-games/); [Project Horseshoe 2017 report, section 3](https://www.projecthorseshoe.com/reports/featured/ph17r3.htm)
- "Safety: A cozy game has an absence of danger and risk. To maximize safety, activities should be voluntary and opt-in so that players never feel the threat of coercion." Abundance: "Lower level Maslow needs (food, shelter) are met or being met ... Nothing is lacking, pressing or imminent." — [Project Horseshoe 2017 report, section 3](https://www.projecthorseshoe.com/reports/featured/ph17r3.htm)
- An academic summary of the report lists the report's cozy visual traits as "a warm and gentle color palette, low ambient lighting, natural material such as wood, stone, water, enclosed spaces." — [Cozy games and their impact, DiVA thesis (2024)](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1881616/FULLTEXT01.pdf), summarising [Project Horseshoe 2017](https://www.projecthorseshoe.com/reports/featured/ph17r3.htm)
- A second secondary source on the report: developers can use "familiar, safe, and warmly lit environments to build trust and autonomy." — [How to Fall in Love With a System: Coziness as a Catalyst for Participatory Agency](https://aw.network/posts/how-to-fall-in-love-with-a-system)

Palette and contrast guidance from UI sources:
- Cozy web/app guidance: warm neutrals (cream, beige, soft browns) with muted accents such as terracotta or honey; avoid "cold, sterile palettes dominated by grays or stark whites." — [Khod, How to Make a Website Look Cozy (8 Design Tips)](https://www.khod.io/resource-center/articles/how-to-make-website-look-cozy)
- A "soft UI" style guide recommends `rounded-2xl` or `rounded-3xl` (Tailwind: 16 px / 24 px) as the primary border radius and pairs soft tones with `slate-500` text "for low contrast harmony"; the same search summary notes this is where accessibility is at risk. — [Soft UI showcase](https://luckymaomi.github.io/ui/styles/soft-ui/showcase/); [UI Style Showcase](https://uistyle.lovable.app/)
- A "cozy game UI" prompt-style guide describes the look as "thick borders, deep shadows, and rounded corners" so "every element feels like a physical object." — [GG Prompts, Cozy Game UI](https://ggprompts.com/styles/cozy-game-ui.html)
- Neumorphism (soft, extruded, same-colour shadows and highlights) "has been criticized for its lack of accessibility, difficulty in implementation, low contrast." — [Wikipedia, Neumorphism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neumorphism)
- Headspace design analysis: the app steers away from pure black and pure white — "pure black creates harsh contrast that conflicts with the calming intent, and pure white reads as clinical." — [Blake Crosley, Headspace: Designing for Calm](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/headspace)
- Headspace brand colours per a third-party design-system page: "sunny yellow and orange, soft cream surfaces, bold geometric type and playful illustrated characters"; values listed include yellow `#FFCD00` and blue `#0061EF`. (Third-party, unverified against Headspace's own materials.) — [Kombai, Headspace design inspiration](https://ww2.kombai.com/gallery/inspirations/headspace/)
- Calm app colours per a generated design spec (weak source): "Moonlit Blue `#1d4763`" (deep teal-blue) and "Forest Green `#2a4a3e`" (muted moss). — [webdesignhot, calm-app design.md](https://www.webdesignhot.com/api/design-md/calm-app.md)
- Peer-reviewed experiment (N = 187, bill-split mobile app, rounded vs angular UI components): rounded corners made the app feel more attractive and warmer, which raised perceived ease of use and satisfaction; the authors define "warm interface aesthetics" as designs that "evoke friendliness and comfort." — [Zhuo et al., Journal of User Experience, May 2025](https://uxpajournal.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/pdf/UXPA_Zhuo_05012025.pdf)
- Pixel-art colour theory (Frogatto): "warm colors pop out to the foreground while cooler colors recede to the background." — [GameDev.net, Pixelcraft: The Colors of Frogatto](https://gamedev.net/blogs/entry/2177585-pixelcraft-the-colors-of-frogatto)
- Community "playful rounded" design-kit spec (not research): "big border radii, pillowy cards, warm friendly colors, rounded typography, and gentle illustrations/mascots." — [wrongstack playful-rounded KIT.md](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@wrongstack/core@0.295.1/design-kits/playful-rounded/KIT.md)

Game-specific colour/light statements:
- A Short Hike: colour palettes were "sampled from Canadian Shield fall photos" and the look combines pixelated rendering with post-processing effects (GDC 2020 Independent Games Summit, "Crafting A Tiny Open World: 'A Short Hike' Postmortem", Adam Robinson-Yu). — [GDC Vault listing](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026613/Independent-Games-Summit-Crafting-A); [Podwise summary of the talk video](https://podwise.ai/episodes/1459231); [Wikipedia, A Short Hike](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/A_Short_Hike); [Uppercut, How A Short Hike Captures the Feeling of Fall](https://uppercutcrit.com/how-a-short-hike-captures-the-feeling-of-fall/)
- A Short Hike's look came from rendering the world as "big crunchy pixels" on simple models, chosen partly because it let a solo developer with limited art skills expand scope. — [Wikipedia, A Short Hike](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/A_Short_Hike)
- A blog links the game's technique to a Unity package that renders at 160x140 px with a 4-colour palette; no developer confirmation of those exact numbers was found. — [Critical Video Game Studies, A Short Hike appreciation post](https://criticalvideogamestudies.com/?p=2445)
- Animal Crossing: New Horizons (CEDEC 2020, art director Koji Takahashi): the art is "balanced between symbolic and realistic"; detail is allocated by an object's role (bugs and fish more realistic, trees and plants more symbolic); "if the picture is too busy, the player becomes passive"; simplification creates an "imaginary gap" that players fill from their own memory, "which leads to creating goals and motives for play." — [GoNintendo summary of the CEDEC 2020 talk](https://gonintendo.com/stories/368281-animal-crossing-new-horizons-dev-team-talks-about-series-innovat); [Adam Norwood, Abstraction and the Meticulous Design of Play in ACNH](https://adamnorwood.com/?p=994); [Nookipedia, Prerelease and unused content](https://www.nookipedia.com/wiki/Prerelease_and_unused_content_in_New_Horizons)
- ACNH's sun is "a literal light source that shifts gradually between day and night," with shadows moving as it does; weather is set per hour and seeded at island creation (forum post by a developer of a similar game, secondhand). — [Bell Tree Forums thread](https://belltreeforums.com/threads/are-there-any-technical-resources-as-to-how-animal-crossing-works-on-a-programming-level.612669)
- Unpacking (Witch Beam; GDC 2023 "'Unpacking' Zen: Designing a Game Without Fail States or Scores", Wren Brier): no scores, no time limits, no fail states, no consequences to choices, no dialogue; "subtractive design" means "strengthening the core of a game by removing anything that isn't serving the core ideas"; "anything we do or don't do on a game is a design choice. And every choice comes at a cost." — [GDC Vault listing](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1029400/-Unpacking-Zen-Designing-a); [Game Developer, Unpacking the design pillars of a chill puzzle game](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/unpacking-the-design-pillars-of-a-chill-puzzle-game); [GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/comments/9269)
- Unpacking is pixel art made in Aseprite (Brier's main tools are Aseprite for pixel art and Photoshop for the rest). — [Uses This interview with Wren Brier](https://www.usesthis.com/interviews/wren.brier)
- Pok Pok Playroom (Apple Design Award, "Delight and Fun"): "low-key, calming environment with multiple brightly colored toys"; "minimalist animations and soothing sounds create a calming feel"; UI is mostly language-free with multi-touch; the team uses "color variables and symbols to keep a minimalist style that helps children feel calm and focus on exploration." — [Sketch blog, Pok Pok](https://sketch.com/blog/pok-pok); [GamesBeat, Pok Pok Playroom](https://gamesbeat.com/pok-pok-playroom-mobile-app-lets-kids-engage-in-open-ended-play/); [Parent Tech podcast, The Pok Pok Interview](https://audible.it/podcast/Parent-Tech/episodes/B0DRNC72LC)
- Toca Boca: the studio "makes toys rather than games" (Emil Ovemar); it reworked Toca Robot Lab because it skewed towards boys and "the color choices and overall aesthetics may have given that impression" (Bjorn Jeffrey, 2014) — the only direct Toca Boca statement on colour found. — [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/articles/052067/r/); [Kidscreen, Toca Boca dishes on gender-neutrality and design](https://kidscreen.com/?p=59849); [Motionographer, The design process behind Toca Boca's apps (2016)](https://motionographer.com/2016/04/27/the-design-process-behind-toca-bocas-infectious-apps/)

### Inferences
- For a 2D pixel room on a phone, the sources translate to: a warm cream/beige page surface rather than white; text in a warm dark brown rather than `#000`; a single warm key light (window beam or lamp) with cooler, desaturated shadow regions so the warm pet and furniture "pop" (Frogatto principle); large radii (16-24 px) on cards and buttons; soft, colour-tinted shadows rather than black drop shadows.
- "Low contrast" in these sources means low *scene* contrast (gentle lighting, muted saturation), not low text contrast. The accessibility criticism of neumorphism and the slate-500 example suggest keeping body text and controls at WCAG minimums while letting the scene itself be soft.
- ACNH's "imaginary gap" and Unpacking's subtractive design both argue for fewer, calmer elements on screen: a busy HUD over the room would work against the cozy read.

### Gaps
- No developer-published hex values or colour-temperature numbers for Animal Crossing, Stardew Valley, A Short Hike, Unpacking, Finch, Pok Pok or Toca Boca were found. The Headspace/Calm hexes above are from third-party or generated pages.
- No source gave a WCAG contrast ratio target specific to "cozy" UI; only the general warning that soft palettes fail contrast.
- The Lost Garden essay's full text could not be fetched; the "warm and gentle color palette, low ambient lighting, natural materials, enclosed spaces" list is quoted via a 2024 thesis summarising the report, not from the report directly.
- Nintendo "Ask the Developer" has no ACNH lighting entry that surfaced; the CEDEC 2020 talk is the primary art-direction source and its lighting/colour remarks were not found in English coverage.

## Key question 2: What motion timings, easings and ambient idle animations do cozy apps use?

### Takeaway
Platform baselines (Material 300 ms medium / 500 ms long with emphasized-decelerate entries; iOS springs defined by duration + bounce, with `.smooth` meaning no bounce) are the only hard numbers with reliable sources. Cozy-specific guidance is qualitative: minimal animation, slow breathing rhythms (Headspace's 4 s in / 6 s out circle), and ambient layered loops (rain, steam, flickering lights, fireflies, snow, lanterns) in pixel scenes. No cozy app or game publishes its tap/sheet durations.

### Cited Findings

Baseline durations and easings:
- Material Components for Android defaults: `motionDurationMedium2` = 300 ms, `motionDurationLong2` = 500 ms; "longer durations suit animations that cover more area." — [material-components-android, docs/theming/Motion.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/theming/Motion.md)
- Material 3 emphasized easing: emphasized `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`, emphasized decelerate `cubic-bezier(0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1)`, emphasized accelerate `cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 0.8, 0.15)`; one table pairs "Emphasized 500 ms" with elements that begin and end on screen and "Emphasized decelerate 400 ms" with elements entering the screen. (Values from the Fluid Qt library docs and third-party M3 summaries; verify against m3.material.io, which could not be fetched.) — [Fluid 1.3.0 MotionEasingTokens](https://docs.liri.io/sdk/fluid/develop/structFluid_1_1MotionEasingTokens.html); [hamen Material 3 skill summary](https://claudeskills.info/zh-hant/skills/hamen/material-3-skill/material-3/); [KPay design docs, Flutter motion](https://design.kpay-group.com/docs/motion/type/flutter-type/flutter-intro)
- Full M3 duration scale reported by third-party snippets: 12 steps from 50 ms (short1) to 600 ms (long4), with medium1 250 ms for standard enter/exit and medium2 300 ms for card expand; the two snippets disagree on mappings. — [Smithery google-material-design skill](https://smithery.ai/skills/copyleftdev/google-material-design); [OpenSkillIndex M3 skill](https://openskillindex.com/skills/intense-visions-harness-engineering-design-material-design-3)
- Apple (WWDC23 "Animate with springs"): springs are defined by two parameters, duration and bounce (bounce range -1.0 to 1.0); SwiftUI's default animation is now a spring; presets can be tuned, e.g. `withAnimation(.snappy(duration: 0.4))`. — [WWDC Notes, Animate with springs](https://wwdcnotes.com/notes/wwdc23/10158)
- Apple's preset descriptions: `.smooth` is "a smooth spring with a predefined duration and no bounce"; `.snappy` has "a small amount of bounce"; `.bouncy` has "a higher amount of bounce." Third-party tables give `.smooth` 0.5 s / bounce 0, `.snappy` 0.3 s / 0.15, `.bouncy` 0.5 s / 0.3 (not in Apple's docs; treat as approximations). — [Apple, SwiftUI Animation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/animation.md); [skillselion animation-patterns](https://skillselion.com/skills/rshankras/claude-code-apple-skills/animation-patterns); [Create with Swift, Understanding Spring Animations](https://www.createwithswift.com/understanding-spring-animations-in-swiftui/)
- `.linear` or `.easeInOut` suit "loading, progressing, or automatically occurring animations"; every animation should respect Reduce Motion. — [Nil Coalescing, SwiftUI animation timing](https://nilcoalescing.com/blog/AnimationTimingInSwiftUI); [skillselion swiftui-animation](https://skillselion.com/skills/dpearson2699/swift-ios-skills/swiftui-animation)

Cozy-app motion:
- Headspace breathing exercises are built on an expanding and contracting circle: expands over 4 seconds, contracts over 6 seconds. (From the German edition of the guide, translated by the search engine.) — [Blake Crosley, Headspace: Design für Gelassenheit](https://blakecrosley.com/de/guides/design/headspace)
- Headspace: "every color, illustration, animation, and transition serving a single emotional objective." — [Blake Crosley, Headspace: Designing for Calm](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/headspace)
- A UX-motion student project on Headspace observed "a simple, static fade-in transition on the Home page" and concluded "the simple animations worked best." — [Marielle, Headspace: UX Motion Design](https://mariellesearlywork.format.com/work-headspace)
- Calm: a generated spec claims "all UI motion follows a 4-second breathing pace" (weak, unverified). — [webdesignhot, calm-app design.md](https://www.webdesignhot.com/api/design-md/calm-app.md)
- Finch: completing a task produces "immediate visible responses: the Finch gains energy, performs joyful animations, or displays messages," credited as strong feedback design. — [Pratt IXD design critique, Finch (2025)](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app-2/); [Pratt IXD design critique, Finch (2026)](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/design-critique-finch-self-care-pet-ios-app/)
- Finch's product-design role works with "character design and character animation" teams, indicating animation is core to the product. — [Finch Care, Mobile Product Designer posting](https://builtindublin.ie/job/mobile-product-designer/4454557)
- Pok Pok: "minimalist animations and soothing sounds create a calming feel." — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/pok-pok-playroom-mobile-app-lets-kids-engage-in-open-ended-play/)
- Unpacking removed everything not serving the core: no timers, scores, fail states; the pace is whatever the player sets. — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/unpacking-the-design-pillars-of-a-chill-puzzle-game)

Ambient idle effects in pixel scenes:
- A commercial lofi pixel-scene pack describes six looping scenes "where each one has many moving layers: rain, steam, flickering lights, fireflies, snow, lanterns and more," drawn at 480x270 and "scaled up by whole numbers only" to keep pixels crisp. — [Pixel Nights animated lofi pixel art loops](https://www.getly.store/product/pixel-nights-animated-lofi-pixel-art-loops-4k-phone-obs-muijfftx)
- A lamp-flicker effect pack: "organically and randomly flickering light animation, ideal for lanterns or candle flames," as a seamless loop. — [Visual Laurence, Gumroad](https://visuallaurence.gumroad.com/l/qkweye)
- A pixel-art particle generator lists "pixelated rain, swirling snow, floating dust motes, or shimmering water" as standard ambient options. — [Pixa, pixel art particle effect generator](https://www.pixa.com/create/pixel-art-particle-effect-generator)
- Asset-pack developer: "glow and some of the effects are done inside the engine with particle effects and are not designed as sprites." — [wishforge.itch.io comments](https://wishforge.itch.io/free-platformer-pixel-art-assets/comments?before=6)
- Pedro Medeiros (Studio Miniboss) publishes 40+ free pixel-art tutorials including "#70 Illumination Techniques", "#28 Light Magic Effects", "#53 Darkness", "#49 Space/stars" at blog.studiominiboss.com/pixelart. — [Session blog, +40 Pixel Art Tutorials](https://blog.usesession.com/blog/itsmeif/180936839366-40-pixel-art-tutorials); [Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/news/artist-makes-animated-gif-tutorials-to-teach-you-pixel-art)

Haptics:
- Apple: "Don't overuse feedback. Overuse can cause confusion and diminish the feedback's significance." "The feedback must match a visual change in the user interface, or must be in response to a user action. Feedback should never come as a surprise." Call `prepare()` before triggering. — [Apple, Playing haptic feedback in your app](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/applepencil/playing-haptic-feedback-in-your-app.md); [Hacking with Swift, UIFeedbackGenerator](https://www.hackingwithswift.com/example-code/uikit/how-to-generate-haptic-feedback-with-uifeedbackgenerator)
- `UIImpactFeedbackGenerator` styles: `.light`, `.medium`, `.heavy`, `.soft` ("gentle, cushioned tap"), `.rigid` ("firm, precise tap"); `UINotificationFeedbackGenerator` `.success/.warning/.error`; `UISelectionFeedbackGenerator` for moving through discrete choices. Impact feedback is meant as a physical "thud" when a view settles into place. — [DEV, Haptic Feedback in iOS: a comprehensive guide](https://dev.to/maxnxi/haptic-feedback-in-ios-a-comprehensive-guide-39fb); [Cocoacasts](https://cocoacasts.com/uikit-fundamentals-adding-haptic-feedback-with-feedback-generators-in-swift); [Microsoft Xamarin iOS haptic feedback docs](https://learn.microsoft.com/nl-nl/previous-versions/xamarin/ios/user-interface/ios-ui/haptic-feedback)
- Situations to skip haptics: "every single tap (overwhelming)" and "scrolling through long lists (battery drain)." — [skillselion haptics reference](https://skillselion.com/skills/johnrogers/claude-swift-engineering/haptics)

### Inferences
- A cozy motion system can be built entirely from platform tokens: ~250-300 ms (M3 medium1/medium2) for taps and small state changes, ~400-500 ms with emphasized-decelerate for sheets and panels entering, and `.smooth`-style springs (no bounce) or at most `.snappy`-level bounce (~0.15) for UI; reserve bouncier springs for the pet's own reactions, where "joyful animations" (Finch) are the point.
- "Breathing" idle UI should run at respiration tempo (Headspace's 4 s/6 s cycle, roughly 6 breaths per minute) rather than at UI speed; the Calm "4-second pace" claim is weak but points the same way.
- For the pixel room, ambient life is cheap and standard: 2-4 looping layers (lamp flicker with random timing, dust motes drifting in the window beam, steam from a bowl, fireflies at night), rendered as engine particles rather than baked frames, at integer scale.
- Haptics: `.soft` or `.light` impact on completing a care task, `.selection` on pickers, `.success` notification only on milestones; nothing on scroll or every tap.

### Gaps
- No cozy app or game (Finch, Pok Pok, Toca Boca, Calm, Headspace, ACNH, Stardew, Unpacking, A Short Hike) publishes its actual tap/sheet/transition durations, easing curves or spring parameters; the numbers above are platform defaults and third-party readings.
- The m3.material.io tokens page could not be fetched; the duration scale and easing beziers are from mirrors and secondary docs and should be verified.
- No tutorial specifically on dust motes in light shafts or fireflies in pixel art was found; only pointers to Medeiros's lighting tutorials and commercial loop packs.
- No source gave haptic *patterns* (Core Haptics intensity/sharpness curves) used by any cozy app.

## Key question 3: How do apps and games do evening or night modes warmly, rather than plain dark mode?

### Takeaway
Reliable material is thin. The clearest evidence is structural: Animal Crossing moves a real sun gradually through the day, Stardew (per community/modder accounts) applies a colour overlay over the same tiles rather than night art, and modders recolour that overlay per season and hour. Dark-mode guidance agrees on avoiding `#000000` in favour of near-blacks (`#0a0a0a`, `#121212`) with a slight warm tint; one design-system example argues the warmth "reduces eye strain and preserves layered depth."

### Cited Findings
- Animal Crossing: New Horizons' sun "is a literal light source that shifts gradually between day and night," shadows move with it; rain turning the sky grey is harder to blend with the day/night cycle (forum post by a developer of a similar game; secondhand). — [Bell Tree Forums](https://belltreeforums.com/threads/are-there-any-technical-resources-as-to-how-animal-crossing-works-on-a-programming-level.612669)
- ACNH's day rolls over at 5 AM local time, separate from the calendar date change at midnight. — [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/117080/how-does-time-work-in-animal-crossing-new-horizons); [Nookipedia, New day](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/New_day)
- ACNH Happy Home Paradise lets players adjust room lighting with a colour and brightness slider ("move left or right to select a colour, which will automatically show in your room"). — [Nintendo Life, HHP lighting guide](https://www.nintendolife.com/guides/animal-crossing-new-horizons-lighting-how-to-change-lighting-in-the-happy-home-paradise-dlc); [GamerGuides](https://venatus.gamerguides.com/animal-crossing-new-horizons/guide/happy-home-paradise-dlc/gameplay/how-to-change-lighting-color)
- Stardew Valley does night "with a filter or shader over the tiles, rather than separate night tiles" (developer forum speculation, not confirmed by ConcernedApe). — [itch.io forum post](https://itch.io/post/13930885)
- Stardew's night timing lives in `Game1` functions `isNightOut`, `getStartingToGetDarkTime`, `getTrulyDarkTime` (patched by the Dynamic Night Time mod, which scales darkness with a multiplier where default = 1 and higher = darker). — [Stardew Forums, Dynamic Night Time](https://forums.stardewvalley.net/resources/dynamic-night-time.41)
- Modders recolour Stardew's ambient light per season and hour: JP's The Ambient Lights "changes the colors of the weather both outdoors and indoors, displaying new shades depending on the time of day and the current season," with intensity and how-dark-nights-get settings; the Ambient Light mod adds a rain colour overlay and a winter scheme. — [CurseForge, JP's The Ambient Lights](https://www.curseforge.com/stardewvalley/mods/jps-the-ambient-lights); [Nexus Mods, Ambient Light #4639](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/4639?tab=logs)
- Players perceive Stardew's palette as heavily yellow; one reply attributes some of it to OS "warmest night mode" removing blue (player guess). — [Stardew Forums, Why yellow?](https://forums.stardewvalley.net/threads/why-yellow-just-why.35821/latest)
- Dark mode: treat `#000000` as prohibited; use a near-black such as `#0a0a0a` for the page and `#171717` for raised surfaces, because pure black creates a "hole in the screen" effect on OLED; "the slight warmth in near-black reduces eye strain and preserves the layered depth that dark mode requires." — [Verdigris design examples, pure black dark mode](https://design.verdigris.co/examples/bad/pure-black-dark-mode)
- Deep greys such as `#121212` "can provide a softer, more comfortable viewing experience"; pure black "should be used sparingly." — [Vocso blog](https://www.vocso.com/blog/?p=32660)
- Headspace avoids pure black because it "creates harsh contrast that conflicts with the calming intent." — [Blake Crosley, Headspace](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/headspace)
- Illustrative warm near-blacks (colour-catalogue pages, not research): `#412100`, `#3F2200`, `#422003`. — [kcolor.co #412100](https://kcolor.co/color/412100); [kcolor.co #3F2200](https://kcolor.co/color/3f2200)

### Inferences
- The pattern for a 2D pixel room is a time-of-day colour overlay (multiply or lerp toward a tint) over unchanged tiles, as Stardew is believed to do, with hour-and-season-keyed tint colours (as the modders implement), plus one or two local warm light sources (lamp, window) that are *excluded* from the darkening so the room reads as "lamp-lit" rather than "dimmed."
- For the surrounding UI chrome at night, the dark-mode sources suggest a warm near-black (around `#0a0a0a`-`#171717` with a brown/amber bias) rather than neutral grey, keeping the same radii and shadows.
- ACNH's 5 AM day boundary is a useful convention: "night" in a pet app can run past midnight until early morning rather than flipping at 00:00.

### Gaps
- No vanilla colour values for Stardew's night tint or Animal Crossing's evening grading were found; they would require decompiling Stardew (`Game1`) or reading mod source.

## Key question 4: What typography conventions do cozy apps and games share?

### Takeaway
Rounded sans (Nunito, Quicksand, Comfortaa, Fredoka) for UI, with Nunito preferred for body because it stays readable at text sizes and Quicksand for larger friendly headings; pixel display faces (Press Start 2P, Silkscreen, Pixelify Sans) reserved for headers and identity, paired with a clean sans for reading. Press Start 2P must be set at multiples of 8 px; Pixelify Sans is the most size-flexible pixel face.

### Cited Findings
- Nunito: a rounded sans with "generous x-height and even proportions [that] maintain excellent readability across sizes"; companion Nunito Sans (same skeleton, straight terminals) works for body text; a common cozy pairing is Nunito headings + Nunito Sans body. — [Made Good Designs, Best Cute Fonts (2026)](https://madegooddesigns.com/cute-fonts/); [Supernotes, 5 best Nunito alternatives](https://supernotes.app/blog/posts/the-5-best-nunito-alternatives/); [Figma fonts, Nunito](https://www.figma.com/fonts/nunito/)
- Quicksand: geometric sans with rounded terminals that "shines best in larger, friendly headings," whereas Nunito "reads comfortably at text sizes." — [Made Good Designs, Nunito vs Quicksand](https://madegooddesigns.com/nunito-vs-quicksand/)
- Comfortaa is almost entirely circular/elliptical forms but "the extreme rounding can reduce legibility at small sizes"; Fredoka is a bolder, bubbly display option. — [Made Good Designs, Best Cute Fonts](https://madegooddesigns.com/cute-fonts/)
- A "playful" trio suggestion: Quicksand + Cabin + Inconsolata. — [fonttrio, friendly collection](https://www.mintlify.com/kapishdima/fonttrio/collections/friendly)
- Press Start 2P is modelled on 1980s Namco arcade type and "works best at sizes of 8px, 16px and other multiples of 8"; it has wide Unicode coverage including Greek and Cyrillic. — [Google Fonts, Press Start 2P](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Press+Start+2P/about); [Figma fonts, Press Start 2P](https://www.figma.com/fonts/press-start-2p/)
- Pixelify Sans is "well-legible at smaller sizes," "can be used in both large and small sizes and maintain readability," and ships in several weights. — [Pixelbuddha, Pixelify Sans](https://pixelbuddha.net/fonts/7611-pixelify-sans-typeface)
- Silkscreen is an all-caps face with extra letter spacing, suited to short headers and labels. — [Speckyboy, free pixel fonts](https://speckyboy.com/free-pixel-fonts.md)
- Celeste-style recommendation: pixel display (Press Start 2P or Silkscreen) for identity, a clean sans (Inter or Nunito) for UI and dialogue; tightly rendered pixel fonts "can be hard for some players to parse, especially at small sizes." — [Made Good Designs, What Font Does Celeste Use?](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=10751)
- Headspace uses "bold geometric type" (third-party). — [Kombai, Headspace](https://ww2.kombai.com/gallery/inspirations/headspace/)
- A cozy "cafe" design system pairs warm neutrals with serif type and "soft typography" (community spec). — [open-design.ai, Cafe design system](https://open-design.ai/systems/cafe/)
- Pok Pok's UI is mostly text-free. — [Sketch blog, Pok Pok](https://sketch.com/blog/pok-pok)

### Inferences
- For PawPixel's existing split (pixel display font for headers, rounded font for UI), the sources support keeping the pixel face to short headers/labels at integer multiples of its native size and never for body copy, with Nunito-class rounded sans at body sizes.
- Fewer words on screen is itself a cozy signal (Pok Pok, Unpacking's "no dialogue"), which argues for icon-plus-short-label UI rather than explanatory text.
