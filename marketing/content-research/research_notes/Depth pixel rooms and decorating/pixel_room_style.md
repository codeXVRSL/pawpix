# "2D pixel art that looks 3D" room style: projection, drawing rules, lighting, characters, coziness (2024-2026)

Research method note: web search worked, but every direct page fetch (WebFetch and curl) was blocked by the sandbox's egress policy for all hosts (slynyrd.com, clip-studio, tutsplus, wikipedia, pixnote, etc.). All findings below therefore come from search-result excerpts of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Where an excerpt was cut off before the relevant section, that is recorded as a gap rather than guessed.

## Key Question 1: Which projection do the viral "cozy pixel room" pieces, the named games and HD-2D use, and what numbers (angles, tile sizes) do tutorials give?

### Takeaway
The viral "isometric pixel room" look (Instagram/Pinterest/itch.io, Habbo, Unpacking, SLYNYRD's cozy-room tutorials) is pixel "isometric", which is really 2:1 dimetric: every receding edge steps 2 px across for 1 px down (about 26.57 degrees, not the true 30 degrees), on floor diamonds of 32x16 or 64x32 px. Stardew/Pokemon-style interiors instead use 3/4 top-down (16x16 tiles, walls about 2 tiles high, no diagonals), and HD-2D is not a pixel projection at all but 2D sprites billboarded inside 3D scenes with tilt-shift and depth of field.

### Cited Findings

Pixel-isometric angle and line rule
- "Traditional isometric is based on 30 degrees angle, but it'd look awfully jaggy for pixel art. Hence for pixel art, we follow 2x1 pixels rule, which approximately 26.565 degrees instead of 30." — [Cheishiru, Clip Studio Tips: Isometric View - Pixel Art Style (+ drawing Room and Store)](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4969)
- The pixel version "runs a 2:1 line at about 26.57°, which is technically dimetric" (arctangent of one half); "two pixels across for every one pixel down". — [the-pixel.art: How Isometric Pixel Art Works (the 2:1 Trick)](https://the-pixel.art/articles/isometric-pixel-art/)
- "Since a true 30º degree line in pixel art results in a jaggy rhythm, isometric pixel art compromises by using the 2:1 line pattern, which is closer to 26.5º." The ground planes of true isometric projection are laid out at 30º angles; there is no vanishing point; tile textures loop on all four sides. — [SLYNYRD Pixelblog 41: Isometric Pixel Art (Nov 2022)](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2022/11/28/pixelblog-41-isometric-pixel-art)
- The 1:2 pixel line gives an angle of exactly 26.5651°; the same chapter also describes a "planometric" pixel perspective where the Y axis is tilted 45° and drawn as a straight 1:1 line. — [OpenGameArt pixel art tutorial, Chapter 3: Perspectives](https://opengameart.org/content/chapter-3-perspectives)
- Kirupa's classic Flash-era lesson describes the slope as "moving one space up or down (y) for every 2 spaces left or right (x)". — [Kirupa: isometric 30 degrees](https://www.kirupa.com/developer/isometric/30degrees.htm)
- Thomas "Cyangmou" Feichtmeir's free 40-chart guide "Isometric games don't exist" (2024) argues that almost no pixel game is true isometric: it distinguishes "true isometric" from "game isometric", states that "Into the Breach is actually not an isometric game", and in v1.4 renamed its "4:3 dimetric iso" chart to "4:3 dimetric rot" so it would not be mistaken for an isometry. — [Cyangmou on itch.io](https://cyangmou.itch.io/isometric-games-dont-exist); [Cyangmou on X](https://x.com/cyangmou/status/1804380141302247582?lang=en); [PixelJoint news post](https://pixeljoint.com/2024/06/16/6980/Pixel_Art_Knowledge_-_Isometric_games_dont_exist.htm)

Tile sizes
- "Most common grid sizes for isometric art are: 32x16 and 64x32. There's no hard rule on tile style and sizes." Tiles can have different heights, "so a wall may be 32x90": the width stays fixed while height varies. — [Clip Studio Tips, Cheishiru](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4969)
- "Each isometric grid space is exactly twice as wide as it is tall"; the most common sizes are "32x16, or 64x32, or 128x64". — [Clint Bellanger: Isometric Tiles Introduction](https://clintbellanger.net/articles/isometric_intro/)
- 64x32 is "the sweet spot for most projects"; "32x16 is a very small size but still useful on mobile devices or if using scaled-up pixel art"; 128x64 for hi-res games. — [sprite-ai.art isometric guide](https://www.sprite-ai.art/guides/isometric-pixel-art)
- SLYNYRD's Pixelblog 41 is described as starting from a 36x36 tile (a square-canvas cube rather than a 2:1 diamond) and says "So long as the top surface is uniform the height of the tiles can vary." — [SLYNYRD Pixelblog 41](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2022/11/28/pixelblog-41-isometric-pixel-art) (36x36 figure comes from a search excerpt; the full article could not be fetched)
- A Godot 4.3 write-up builds its tiles with a 4-pixel step at the top and bottom edge of the diamond (tile style with a flat 4 px nub at the vertices). — [Stephan Bester, Isometric tiles for a pixel art game in Godot 4.3](https://stephan-bester.medium.com/isometric-tiles-for-a-pixel-art-game-in-godot-4-3-94b09846c9df)
- Anecdotal tip (unverified, single itch.io post): 64x32 "leaves gaps or overlaps" and the poster recommends 64x31 or 32x15 diamonds. — [itch.io blog: Isometric Pixelart Info](https://itch.io/blog/1529126/isometric-pixelart-info)
- SLYNYRD distinguishes cube/tile styles by how many pixels of flat step the top and bottom vertices have: "the D1 style adheres more to the 2:1 grid and tiles perfectly, but those sharp corners are unnerving to me"; wide corners look better but "require a bit of clever overlapping to tile well". — [SLYNYRD Pixelblog 41](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2022/11/28/pixelblog-41-isometric-pixel-art)

Unpacking (Witch Beam, 2021)

### Inferences
- For a 130x100 to 160x120 px room, the practical choice is the 32x16 diamond (an 8x6-tile floor in 2:1 iso is 128+ px wide), with every diagonal stepped exactly 2 px across per 1 px down; 64x32 does not fit at that canvas size. The 36x36 SLYNYRD cube and the 4 px "flat nub" tile style are variants of the same 2:1 grid, differing only in how the diamond's left/right vertices are drawn.
- Unpacking is "isometric" in the loose game sense (devs and reviewers use the word); nothing found confirms whether its receding lines are 2:1. The dev detail that furniture was split into pieces to fake depth, and that items are drawn in multiple angles, is the actionable part: depth comes from layering pieces, not from a 3D engine.
- Stardew/Pokemon interiors are the alternative "3/4 top-down" family: one flat back wall about 2 tiles high, no side walls shown, floor as a plain grid; tutorials for it never mention diagonals, so it is cheaper to code but reads less "3D" than the two-back-wall iso room.
- HD-2D is a 3D-rendering recipe (billboards + tilt-shift + DoF) and only its mood cues (warm point lights, blurred foreground/background to make a "miniature") transfer to a flat procedural room.

## Key Question 2: What are the drawing rules for a convincing isometric room (wall thickness, floor grid, line angles, shading tones, outline choice, depth sorting)?

### Takeaway
Tutorials converge on: build the room as a cube on the 2:1 grid (Clip Studio's example is a 2x2-tile floor, 2 tiles high on a 32x16 grid, walls made taller by adding canvas), keep verticals perfectly vertical, keep floor and wall edges on the grid so tiles meet without seams, shade the three planes top/left/right with three tones, use dark coloured (not black) selective outlines or none, and draw everything back-to-front by tile row then column (painter's algorithm, depth = x + y + height).

### Cited Findings

Room geometry
- Clip Studio room example: build the room "as a cube, with 2x2 tiles for the floor and 2 tiles high" on a 32x16 grid; walls can be made taller "by adding canvas height"; you "should be able to see the entire room on the canvas". On wall/tile edges: "putting a straight line for the edge might look awkward, but when you color it, the sides looks just right." Workflow: base colours first, then shadows, then highlights. — [Clip Studio Tips, Cheishiru](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4969)
- pixnote's room guidance: "everything in a room obeys the same rules"; verticals stay vertical; one light direction chosen early and never changed. — [pixnote: Isometric Pixel Art Guide](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/isometric/)
- Tuts+ isometric room tutorial builds the base from L-shaped 2:1 lines. — [Tuts+: How to Create an Isometric Pixel Art Room](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-create-an-isometric-pixel-art-room-in-adobe-photoshop--cms-28573)
- Tuts+ house/apartment tutorials: use the character as a yardstick to find a reasonable floor height; build a wall from stacked 2:1 lines with "each line 4px above the previous" (house) or "three blank pixels between each line" (apartment) so later textures such as brick or wood align. — [Tuts+: Isometric Pixel Art House](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/create-an-isometric-pixel-art-house-in-adobe-photoshop--cms-22088); [Tuts+: Apartment Building](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/create-an-isometric-pixel-art-apartment-building-in-adobe-photoshop--cms-23006); [Tuts+: Coffee Shop](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/create-an-isometric-pixel-art-coffee-shop-in-adobe-photoshop--cms-23828)
- Cube as the atom: "The cube is the foundation of isometric art. Every structure can be built from cube combinations"; draw a diamond from a centre point with 2:1 diagonals, then raise it. — [sprite-ai.art isometric guide](https://www.sprite-ai.art/guides/isometric-pixel-art)
- A cube "is three flat shapes stuck together at the right angles"; shade three faces (top light, one side mid, one dark) to sell volume. — [the-pixel.art](https://the-pixel.art/articles/isometric-pixel-art/)
- Drububu: finish outline first, then shade; "by using a shaded isometric cube, the shading needs to be done only once (and then copied many times)"; keep the cube tileable because some cube styles "can't properly be stacked vertically; the vertical lines don't fit". — [Drububu pixel art tutorial](https://www.drububu.com/tutorial/)
- Tiling: "a single pixel off creates visible seams when tiles are placed next to each other". — [sprite-ai.art](https://www.sprite-ai.art/guides/isometric-pixel-art)
- Tile textures must loop on all four sides; tile height may vary as long as the top surface is uniform. — [SLYNYRD Pixelblog 41](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2022/11/28/pixelblog-41-isometric-pixel-art)
- Walls/floors in Habbo are small repeating patterns. — [Habborator](https://habborator.org/art); a hobby renderer uses a 12 px floor-slab thickness under 64x32 tiles. — [CodePen](https://codepen.io/veltix/pen/ZrjPYv)
- Engine practice: keep floor on a lower sort layer than walls (separate tilemaps); mismatched sprite pivots between wall and floor tiles cause seams. — [Unity discussion: Build isometric room](https://discussions.unity.com/t/build-isometric-room/836741)
- Shadow-casting geometry only works on a flat ground plane in iso because there is no horizon or vanishing point. — [Pixel Parmesan: Fundamentals of Isometric Pixel Art](https://pixelparmesan.com/blog/fundamentals-of-isometric-pixel-art)

Outlines
- Lospec, "Pixel Art Outlines Part 2: Using Color": selective outlining ("sel-out") changes the outline colour across the sprite: dark shades get a dark outline, lit areas a lighter one; consider the background colour. — [Lospec](https://lospec.com/articles/pixel-art-outlines-part-2-using-color/); five-part sel-out series by Preston Kyle Mays. — [Lospec tutorials](https://lospec.com/pixel-art-tutorials/the-pixel-zone-selective-outlining-by-preston-kyle-mays)
- pixnote's four-step sel-out: wrap the silhouette in a hard dark outline, then replace the outline on the lit side with a lighter body colour; "a darker version of the body's darkest tone often works better than pure black". — [pixnote: Outlines and Anti-Aliasing](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/outlines/)
- "Colored outlines work better than black ones in many cases ... use an outline that's a darker shade of the object they're outlining ... maintaining contrast" is the key. — [OpenGameArt pixel art tutorial part 3](https://opengameart.org/forumtopic/oga-pixel-art-tutorial-part-3-the-basics)
- Professional order of work: shading and volume first, "the outlines (if there are any) are added at the end". — [OpenGameArt comment thread](https://opengameart.org/comment/29446)
- Tsugumo's classic chapter: sel-out keeps a sprite readable against busy backgrounds. — [Yarrninja chapter 12](http://www.yarrninja.com/pixeltutorial/chapter12.htm)

Depth sorting
- "The secret behind successful isometric sorting is to make use of the Painter's Algorithm ... draw those objects higher up the screen first (lowest y value)"; there is a limit to how well a 2D sprite system can do this. — [Parallel Realities isometric tutorial](https://www.parallelrealities.co.uk/tutorials/isometric/isometric1.php)
- Row-major order: walk the grid rows; "any element in the second row will have a higher sortingOrder than any element of the first row". — [Tuts+: Isometric depth sorting for moving platforms](https://code.tutsplus.com/isometric-depth-sorting-for-moving-platforms--cms-30226t)
- Per-object key: depth = gridX + gridY (+ small height term); collect tiles and sprites into one list and sort once per frame so a character can be in front of one tile and behind another; for differently sized sprites use the corner nearest the camera, not the origin. — [GameDev.net: isometric depth sorting](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/470599-isometric-depth-sorting/)
- Split tall sprites into pieces at different height layers so characters can pass in front of one part and behind another. — [Tiled forum: Isometric depth sorting](https://discourse.mapeditor.org/t/isometric-depth-sorting/736)
- Godot 4.3: set TileMapLayer shape to isometric and enable Y-sort so tiles lower on screen draw over higher ones. — [Stephan Bester, Godot 4.3](https://stephan-bester.medium.com/isometric-tiles-for-a-pixel-art-game-in-godot-4-3-94b09846c9df)
- "If a body covers three tiles, your depth sorting will be a nightmare": keep character footprints to one tile. — [sprite-ai.art](https://www.sprite-ai.art/guides/isometric-pixel-art)
- Unpacking split furniture into separate pieces to create the illusion of depth. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/intimacy-from-the-inanimate-in-house-moving-puzzler-unpacking)

### Inferences
- A code recipe consistent with all sources: floor diamond of W x H tiles at 32x16 (or 24x12 for a smaller room; still 2:1), two back walls rising straight up from the back-left and back-right floor edges, wall height a round multiple of the tile's half-height (16, 24 or 32 px) chosen against the character sprite; skirting as a 1-2 px darker band at the wall/floor join; wallpaper and floor as 2:1-sheared repeating patterns; a 2-4 px wall "thickness" strip on the outer edge (hobby Habbo renderers use a visible floor slab, 12 px at 64x32 scale, i.e. about 6 px at 32x16).
- Outlines: the room shell (walls/floor) is usually lineless with plane-colour contrast; small furniture and the pet benefit from a selective outline in a darker hue of the object's own colour, never pure black, so it stays readable against patterned walls.
- Draw order for a procedural renderer: floor, back walls, then objects sorted by (row + column), with the pet inserted into the same list using its feet tile; split tall furniture (bookcase, bed) into base and top if the pet must walk behind it.

### Gaps
- No tutorial excerpt gave a numeric skirting/baseboard height or wall-thickness in pixels; these must be chosen by eye.
- Pixelblog 54's step-by-step room construction (the most directly relevant source) could not be read.

## Key Question 3: How are shadows and lighting handled so flat sprites read as 3D?

### Takeaway
One fixed light from the upper-left (or upper-right) chosen first, three planes in three tones (top brightest, left medium, right darkest), shadows hue-shifted cooler (toward blue/purple) and highlights warmer (toward yellow) by roughly 20° per ramp step with a 20-25% value drop, and a hard-edged cast shadow projected from the object's footprint along the 2:1 ground plane away from the light.

### Cited Findings
- "A light source should be established at the very start of any illustration. This will dictate the flow of shadows, tell you where highlights should be placed, and otherwise guide you with selecting color"; the author most often puts primary light "from the top right or left hand side of the composition"; pixel art "has a tendency to appear flat if light is not handled with care". — [SLYNYRD Pixelblog 6: Light and Shadow](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/6/15/pixelblog-6-light-and-shadow)
- pixnote's cube: "Fill colors: Top → bright, left → medium, right → dark"; light from upper-left with shadows to lower-right is "the most common pattern"; keep it consistent across all objects. — [pixnote isometric guide](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/isometric/)
- pixnote shading guide: shadow stages by canvas size, "2 stages for 16x16 and 3 stages for 32x32"; shadows should shift cool; hue shifting is "the most important technique for attractive pixel art shadows"; avoid pillow shading (even shading along the whole outline is "the most common beginner mistake"). — [pixnote: Shading and Lighting](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/shading)
- Light is typically top-left in isometric scenes, so the top surface is lightest and the right face darkest. — [sprite-ai.art isometric guide](https://www.sprite-ai.art/guides/isometric-pixel-art)
- Hue shifting defined as rotating hue at every ramp step; suggested start "about 20 degrees of hue per step", highlights drifting toward yellow and shadows toward blue or purple; saturation peaks in the middle of the ramp; warm light + cool shadow is the default but moonlight, neon and overcast scenes flip it. (Page updated Oct 2026.) — [the-pixel.art: Hue Shifting in Pixel Art](https://the-pixel.art/articles/hue-shifting-pixel-art-shading/)
- A good ramp has "the hue shifting by positive degrees as the brightness increases". — [SLYNYRD Pixelblog 1: Color Palettes](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/1/10/pixelblog-1-color-palettes)
- Shadows lean cool (purple) and highlights warm (yellow) because the shadow side is lit by ambient sky, not the direct source; hue shifting becomes essential at 6-8 step ramps. — [pixel-editor.com: Color Theory for Pixel Art](https://www.pixel-editor.com/articles/color-theory-for-pixel-art)
- Stardew modding wiki: shift shadows to a cooler hue and highlights to a warmer hue. — [Stardew Modding Wiki: How to make pixel art](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/How_to_make_pixel_art)
- Three-tone rules of thumb from several 2024-2026 guides (exact attribution per number not verified, since pages could not be fetched): three tones (shadow, base, light) is enough for a 16x16 character; give each material 2-3 shades and think per material not per sprite; drop shadow value 20-25% from base (10% becomes indistinguishable at distance); keep highlight to about one sixth of the base area; total hue shift across a three-tone ramp about 50-60°; 4-8 colours total for tiny 16 px icons. — [DEV: Pixel Art Color Palette Guide](https://dev.to/krila_software/the-pixel-art-color-palette-guide-how-to-choose-colors-that-work-1bg7); [art-pia: Light and Shadow](https://art-pia.com/guide-light-shadow-en.html); [art-pia: Color Palette](https://art-pia.com/guide-color-palette-en.html); [Pixel Grimoire: Basic Shading](https://medium.com/pixel-grimoire/how-to-start-making-pixel-art-4-f57f51dcfa02)
- Cast shadows in iso: "project lines from the bottom edges of the form at the same angle along the ground plane", connect the endpoints; the angle sets light direction and line length sets light elevation; rounded objects should be built inside isometric boxes (the ellipse's information is at its vertices). — [Pixel Parmesan: Fundamentals of Isometric Pixel Art](https://pixelparmesan.com/blog/fundamentals-of-isometric-pixel-art)
- Isometric shadow tiles can be made by darkening, cropping and blurring a copy and skewing it onto the iso ground. — [Screaming Brain Studios: Isometric Shadows](https://screamingbrainstudios.com/isometric-shadows/)
- A drop shadow is "a copy in black or gray of the object, drawn in a slightly different position"; soft edges are common. — [Wikipedia: Drop shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drop_shadow)
- Lamp lighting in a room: "a warm patch on the floor and a shadow behind a nearby block make that lamp belong to a room"; the shadow "should continue away from the source and touch the object that casts it". — [pixel-editor.com: Pixel Art Lighting and Glow Effects](https://www.pixel-editor.com/articles/pixel-art-lighting-effects)
- Dark-room palettes: use dark blues, deep purples or desaturated charcoals instead of pure black for shadows, and a small light source such as a monitor glow or bedside lamp; "a single, sharp light source, such as a window pane, can create dramatic long shadows across the floor". — [Dark Colored Bedroom Pixel Art guide](https://origin-exchange.autodesk.com/dark-colored-bedroom-pixel-art) (low-authority blog)
- Game-engine window light: a greyscale "cookie" texture masks the light so the window frame casts shadows on the floor. — [xDasher devlog: 4 things we did to add light and depth](https://pixel-beef.itch.io/xdasher/devlog/192949/4-things-we-did-to-add-light-and-depth-to-our-pixel-art-game)
- HD-2D uses point lights so sprites cast real shadows; depth of field and tilt-shift give the diorama feel. — [Wikipedia: HD-2D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD-2D)

### Inferences
- For a procedural renderer: pick light = upper-left once; derive each object's three plane tones from its base colour by (value -20..25%, hue -20° toward blue) for the right/dark face and (value +, hue +20° toward yellow) for the top; cast shadows as a hard 1-tone darker overlay of the footprint diamond offset 2 px right / 1 px down (i.e. along the 2:1 ground line away from the light), kept within the floor.
- Shadows must touch the base of the object that casts them; floating shadows are the most common tell of "pasted-on" sprites.
- A window "sun patch" is a lighter, warmer parallelogram on the floor (sheared 2:1) plus the frame's bars drawn in the floor-shadow tone; a lamp is a warm radial patch of 1-2 lighter tones around its base.

### Gaps
- No tutorial gave a pixel offset for drop shadows under furniture or characters; offsets above are inferred.
- SLYNYRD Pixelblog 6's sections on tone count and hue shift were not reachable.

## Key Question 4: How are front-facing or 3/4 chibi character sprites placed in isometric rooms without looking wrong?

### Takeaway
Sources agree the character's feet (ground point) must sit exactly on a tile centre or intersection, its footprint should be one tile, it should be about two tile-heights (about 32 px on 32x16 / 16x16 scales), and it is sorted with the furniture by its foot tile. Habbo and most iso games avoid the "front-facing sprite" problem by drawing the figure in the diagonal directions (8 for Habbo); no source documents a rule for dropping a purely front-facing sprite into an iso room, which SLYNYRD's "don't mix projections" guidance warns against.

### Cited Findings
- "The feet, or 'ground point,' should sit exactly on tile intersections. Get that wrong and they'll look like they're hovering." Isometric characters are drawn wider and shorter than side-view sprites; feet slightly wider than legs, one foot lower on screen than the other; a symmetrical character "only requires the two diagonal front and back views". — [Pixel Parmesan: Fundamentals of Isometric Pixel Art](https://pixelparmesan.com/blog/fundamentals-of-isometric-pixel-art)
- "32x32 for small characters on 64x32 tiles ... feet should fit comfortably on one tile. Head can extend beyond, that's fine"; a body covering three tiles makes depth sorting "a nightmare". — [sprite-ai.art isometric guide](https://www.sprite-ai.art/guides/isometric-pixel-art)
- Character sprite about 32 px tall on 16x16 tiles, "about 2 16x16 px tile heights" in most motions. — [itch.io community post](https://itch.io/post/2820482)
- Pseudo-iso minifig draft: figures "roughly 4 units high by 2 units wide". — [OpenGameArt: pseudo-iso minifig sprites](https://opengameart.org/content/pseudo-iso-minifig-sprites-draft-in-progress)
- Stardew: 16x32 NPC sprites on 16x16 tiles (2 tiles tall, 1 tile footprint). — [Stardew Valley forums](https://forums.stardewvalley.net/threads/sprite-sizes-character-sheets-pixel-art.5597/)
- Habbo avatars are rendered in 8 directions (0-7 clockwise) with layered parts. — [DEV Community: Habbo Avatar Rendering Basics](https://dev.to/trickstival/habbo-avatar-rendering-basics-4cg6)
- Tuts+ uses the character as the yardstick for building and floor heights. — [Tuts+ coffee shop tutorial](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/create-an-isometric-pixel-art-coffee-shop-in-adobe-photoshop--cms-23828)
- Projections "should not be mixed in the same scene". — [SLYNYRD Pixelblog 3](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/3/14/pixelblog-3-graphical-projections-1); 3/4 top-down characters show a large portion of front and top. — [SLYNYRD Pixelblog 55](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2025/3/24/pixelblog-55-top-down-character-animation)
- Sorting a moving character: compute its depth from its tile (x + y) and sort with tiles in one list; split tall objects so the character can be in front of one part and behind another. — [GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/470599-isometric-depth-sorting/); [Tiled forum](https://discourse.mapeditor.org/t/isometric-depth-sorting/736)

### Inferences
- A front-facing or 3/4 chibi pet (as in PawPixel's 41x34 canvas) can sit in a 2:1 iso room if: (1) its feet/paws rest on the centre of a floor diamond (bottom-centre of the sprite = tile centre), (2) it has a flattened elliptical or diamond shadow the size of one tile under the paws (the iso footprint), (3) it is at most about 2 tile-heights tall relative to the room's tile (for 32x16 tiles that is about 32 px, which is close to the 34 px chibi), and (4) it is inserted into the same row+column painter's sort as furniture. The shadow is what reconciles the projections: a 2:1 ellipse tells the eye the figure stands on the iso floor even though the body is drawn front-on, which is why Habbo-style rooms read fine with chunky front-leaning avatars.
- Keep the sprite's silhouette symmetric and its base wide; the "wider and shorter" iso-character guidance means a chibi with a big head and short legs already fits the convention better than a tall side-view sprite would.

### Gaps
- No source explicitly documents a "front-facing sprite in an isometric room" technique (Habbo, Pengu, cozy pet games); the shadow-ellipse and feet-placement rule above is inferred from iso-character guidance, not from a Habbo/Pengu writeup.
- No Habbo avatar pixel height or shadow spec was found.

## Key Question 5: What makes rooms "cozy" visually (palette, clutter, warm light, window glow, small animated details)?

### Takeaway
Cozy pixel rooms use a restricted warm earth-tone palette (about 16-32 colours), a cool/dark base with warm light sources (lamps, windows) as focal points, rounded forms and natural materials, enclosed intimate framing, and slow small looping animations (flicker, steam, blink) rather than big motion.

### Cited Findings
- Academic (2026): earth tones and warm colours define the cozy genre's look; "earth colors provide the signature palette, such as yellow ochre and warm browns" (Stardew Valley as main example); Animal Crossing keeps warm tones "blended into a diffused optical softness"; in Cozy Grove "colour is instrumental to gameplay and operates as an important and impactful feedback mechanism", using warm/cool and desaturation. — [Krzywinska et al., Coziness in Games, Games and Culture (SAGE)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15554120241310920)
- Cozy design traits: "warmer, low-contrast colors and lighting, natural and familiar building materials, enclosed intimate spaces". — [Game Developer: Designing for Coziness](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-for-coziness)
- Warm colours, rounded shapes, nature, pets and handmade details create the inviting mood. — [GameTyrant: Why Cozy Games Became a Serious Trend](https://gametyrant.com/news/why-cozy-games-became-a-serious-gaming-trend)
- Practical cozy-scene recipe: restrict to 16-32 colours; cool background (midnight navy, slate grey) with warm hues saved for light sources so the temperature contrast "creates a sense of shelter"; start around 128x128 or 256x128 px; use dithering for rugs/blankets; suggest small items with only 2-3 pixels. — [BudgetPixel: How to Create a Cozy Pixel Art Scene in 5 Steps](https://budgetpixel.com/blog/how-to-create-a-cozy-pixel-art-scene-in-5-steps)
- Window glow: fill window panes with "a light and saturated yellow-orange", add a light emission, and keep it restrained ("less is more"). — [CodeAndWeb SpriteIlluminator tutorial](https://www.codeandweb.com/spriteilluminator/tutorials/how-to-create-dynamically-lit-2d-scenes-for-unity); gallery examples describe "a warm yellow-orange light emanating through the windows, suggesting interior activity". — [pixie.haus cozy bookstore](https://pixie.haus/pixelart/607825/cozy-bookstore-facade-night-glow)
- Dark cozy bedrooms: shadows in dark blue/purple/charcoal rather than black, one small warm light source (monitor, bedside lamp). — [Dark Colored Bedroom Pixel Art](https://origin-exchange.autodesk.com/dark-colored-bedroom-pixel-art) (low-authority)
- Unpacking is described as "a digital doll's house". — [Disegno Journal: Let's Unpack That](https://disegnojournal.com/newsfeed/unpacking-cosy-video-games)
- Animation: keep movement slow so pixel edges hold up; examples are flickering lanterns, drifting fireflies, idle bobs. — [Kling: animate pixel art](https://kling.ai/topics/animate-pixel-art-ai); a two-frame idle with a 1 px vertical shift, or four frames for breathing. — [sprite-ai.art: how to animate pixel art](https://www.sprite-ai.art/guides/how-to-animate-pixel-art); blinking "shouldn't be too frequent", stretched over an 8-frame cycle, with secondary motion (hair) for life. — [itch.io: Hero Idle Animation tutorial](https://itch.io/t/2489389/pixel-tutorial-hero-idle-animation); Endesga idle quick-tip on Lospec. — [Lospec](https://lospec.com/pixel-art-tutorials/pixelart-quicktip-idle-animation-by-endesga); offset a secondary part (hair/tail) by a frame so it lags the body. — [Sandro Maglione: Pixel art character animations guide](https://www.sandromaglione.com/articles/pixel-art-character-animations-guide)
- Market signals for the cozy iso room: ToffeeCraft's kit ships many recolours of bed/carpet/chair/desk; Still Rooms lists "warm rustic homes ... compact student rooms". — [ToffeeCraft](https://toffeecraft.itch.io/cozy-room-pixel-asset-pack); [Steam: Still Rooms](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1314430/Still_Rooms/)

### Inferences
- For PawPixel's room: one warm key light (window by day, lamp by night) painted as a lighter warm patch on the floor and wall, everything else in a slightly cool, desaturated ramp; a palette of roughly 16-32 colours shared across walls, floor, furniture and pet; many small 2-3 px props (books, mugs, plants) rather than few large ones; 2-4 frame loops at low speed for a candle/lamp flicker, steam, plant sway and the pet's blink/tail.
- Recolourable furniture (the ToffeeCraft pattern) is the cheap way to give decorating variety procedurally: one shape, several palette ramps.

### Gaps
- No source quantified "clutter density" (objects per tile or per room) for cozy scenes; the small-prop guidance is qualitative.
- No source analysed Unpacking's palette or lighting specifically.
