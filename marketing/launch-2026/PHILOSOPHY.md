# Kept Light

**A design philosophy for small, repeated acts of attention.**

Kept Light treats the pixel as a unit of care. Every square on the canvas stands for one small thing done on time: a bowl filled, a door opened, a name spoken. Nothing is decorative by accident. A composition is a ledger of attention, built square by square, and the eye should feel the patience in it before it understands the subject. Work in this manner looks meticulously crafted, as if each square were placed by hand over many evenings by someone at the very top of the craft.

Space is warm and generous. Large fields of cream and sand hold a single living subject, the way a quiet room holds a sleeping animal. The subject sits low and close to the floor line, never floating; weight and gravity are part of tenderness. Margins are wide and exact. Negative space is not empty but held, like a breath before a name is called. Every alignment snaps to one underlying grid, so that even the air around the subject feels counted.

Colour is limited and domestic: cream, sand, ink, and one coral accent that appears only where the heart of the image is. Dusk lavender and night plum are allowed as a second chapter, never mixed into the first. Shadows are flat bands, not gradients; light is a soft glow behind the subject, painted as concentric steps of the background colour. The palette should feel calibrated through painstaking trial, each tone earning its place against its neighbours.

Scale carries the story. The same creature appears at three magnitudes: a photograph, a field of coloured squares, a tiny figure. Sequences read left to right like an observation log, with small reference marks, plate numbers and hairline rules that suggest a field guide from an imaginary discipline devoted to the study of being looked after. Repetition is the rhythm: rows of the same figure in different moods, grids of swatches, ticks along an edge. Reward the long look.

Type is a whisper beside the image. A blocky display face for the one phrase that matters, set large and alone; a quiet rounded sans for anything else, small and sparse. Labels behave like specimen tags, uppercase, widely tracked, hairline thin. Words never explain what the image already shows. Nothing touches the edge, nothing overlaps, and every element sits on the grid with master-level precision, the product of deep expertise and countless careful passes.
